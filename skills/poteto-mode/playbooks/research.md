### Research

**You own the decision's evidence.** Use for external, domain, or dependency questions whose answer informs a decision. For current code behavior, use [Investigation](investigation.md). For historical motivation, call the Skill tool with `why`. No PR for the research answer itself.

1. **Framed.** The question, the freshness it needs, the evidence that would answer it, and the stopping point are written down. Answer a single lookup directly. Call the Skill tool with `swarm` to split independent domains only when separate readers would help.
2. **Sourced.** Every material claim cites a primary source at the place it appears, with its version, date, and conditions. Read primary sources before relying on summaries. Check the relevant version, date, and conditions. State limits that could change the decision. Count independent observations, not pages that repeat one origin.
3. **Challenged.** The conclusion has survived a search for evidence that could overturn it, and the contradictions and unknowns are still on the page. Preserve contradictions and unknowns using the confidence and contradiction guidance in [Why's epistemics reference](../../why/references/epistemics.md). A failed lookup or missing metadata does not establish that a capability is absent. Support a negative claim with an explicit statement or a check that could disprove it, and name its scope.
4. **Settled.** The findings answer the framed question, or the remaining gap and what would close it are named. Compare findings against the framed question. Resolve conflicts where the evidence permits. Report any that remain. Stop when the evidence answers the decision or further progress needs unavailable evidence.
5. **Written.** The reader can make the decision from the brief alone. Give the answer or recommendation, supporting sources, tradeoffs, and material uncertainties. Keep facts distinct from inference. Call the Skill tool with `unslop`. Save a cited Markdown brief when later work needs it. Use the requested path when given. Otherwise use the repository's established research directory, or create `docs/research/<topic>.md` in the workspace when there is none.

**Reply:** the decision, evidence, limits, and brief path when saved.
